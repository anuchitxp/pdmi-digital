# Protocol and Implementation Framework

---

## 1. Introduction: Gaps of LDL-C Control in ACS Patients

*(ช่องว่างในการบรรลุเป้าหมาย LDL-C ในผู้ป่วย ACS)*

### Current Situation in Thailand

- Only **25%** of ACS patients can achieve **LDL-C < 70 mg/dL** within 4 months after hospital discharge.
- Rapid reduction of LDL-C to **<55 mg/dL** early after ACS onset can reduce MACE to only **3%**, compared to **11-12%** with conventional treatment.

### Barriers to Achieving LDL-C Targets

The low proportion of post-ACS patients reaching LDL-C goals stems from barriers related to patients, healthcare professionals, and the healthcare system in Thailand.

#### Patient-Related Barriers
- Delayed initiation of medication
- Concerns about side effects, leading to non-adherence
- Lack of knowledge and understanding about treatment
- Desire for stricter lipid control but lack confidence in achieving it
- Financial constraints affecting access to treatment

#### HCP-Related Barriers
- Concerns about medication side effects
- Limited time for patient assessment and follow-up
- Inconsistent or unsuccessful dose titration
- Complex patient risk profiles making disease management difficult
- Desire for better outcomes but facing multiple limitations

#### Healthcare System-Related Barriers
- Limited access to effective medications due to insurance or reimbursement restrictions

## 2. The Concept of PDMI Protocol: A Tool for ACS Care

*(แนวคิดของ PDMI protocol: เครื่องมือสำหรับการดูแลผู้ป่วย ACS)*

### National Protocol Consensus for Post-ACS Discharge and Follow-up in Thailand

**Objective:** To establish a standard protocol for post-ACS care from discharge through the first year of follow-up to reduce morbidity and mortality.

### Overview of Key Steps Leading to Discharge

#### Individualised Plan for Patients
- Give a written discharge plan to patients
- Explain the reason for hospitalisation
- Inform and educate regarding purpose of medications
- Assess patient's understanding

#### Discharge Preparation
- Generate a clear follow-up plan based on patient's condition
- Coordinate appointments for clinical follow-up and post-discharge laboratory testing

#### Patient and Family Education
- Educate on cardiac symptom recognition
- Educate on secondary risk factor prevention
- Provide educational tools

#### Follow-up Plan Discussion with Patients
- Review appointment plans with patient
- Send discharge summary to patient's primary care provider after discharge
- Telephone the patient 2-3 days after discharge to address concerns

#### Next Visits
- Review whether patient has achieved secondary prevention treatment goals
- Intensified treatment/dose titration if needed

## 3. Mandatory and Optional Activities for Post-Discharge Care Plan

Activities for follow-up after discharge, starting from the first visit within 30 days and continuing every 3-6 months for up to 1 year.

### Mandatory Activities (at each visit)
- Measure ECG/CXR
- Conduct blood tests including lipid profile*
- Reconcile and optimize medications
- Educate patient and caregiver on medication plan and lifestyle modification
- Schedule follow-up appointment

### Optional Activities (as needed)
- Optimize medication, if needed
- Conduct blood tests including lipid profile*
- Educate patient and caregiver on medication plan and lifestyle modification
- Schedule follow-up appointment

> \*Re-evaluate lipid levels 4–6 weeks after each treatment or dose adjustment to assess treatment response and check for safety issues.

### Additional Recommendations
- Include patient assessment, physical activity counselling, exercise training (central component of cardiac rehabilitation), diet/nutritional counselling, risk factor control, patient education, psychosocial management, and vocational advice.
- Make telephone contact every 3 weeks (more frequent telephone contact within 30 days after discharge may be needed).
- If possible, conduct monthly group discussions between healthcare professionals, primary healthcare providers, and patients and/or caregivers at community-based cardiac rehabilitation centres.

## 4. Clinical Practice Recommendations for Post-ACS Care in Thailand

### Antiplatelet Management

| Statement | Recommendation |
|-----------|----------------|
| 1 | Dual antiplatelet therapy (aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor) should be prescribed in all post-ACS patients for up to 12 months unless contraindicated. |
| 2 | The use of prasugrel for up to 12 months should be confined to patients receiving percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). |

### Lipid Management

| Statement | Recommendation |
|-----------|----------------|
| 3 | The highest tolerated dose of statin should be initiated as soon as possible and continued long term to reduce cardiovascular events, regardless of the initial LDL value. |
| 4 | Ezetimibe and/or PCSK9 inhibitors should be considered in patients who cannot achieve LDL target despite highest-tolerated statin dose, or in patients who cannot tolerate statin therapy. |

### Blood Pressure Management

| Statement | Recommendation |
|-----------|----------------|
| 5 | ACEIs (or ARBs if intolerant) should be initiated and continued long term in all post-ACS patients, regardless of left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF). |
| 6 | β-blockers should be initiated in post-ACS patients who have LVEF ≤40%, regardless of HF symptoms. The use beyond 12 months among patients without reduced LVEF, angina, arrhythmias, or uncontrolled hypertension should be reassessed. |
| 7 | A mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (e.g., eplerenone) is recommended for post-ACS patients with LVEF ≤40% and HF or type 2 diabetes (T2D). |

### Blood Glucose Management (Type 2 Diabetes)

| Statement | Recommendation |
|-----------|----------------|
| 8 | The HbA1c target should be individualised to avoid hypoglycaemia. |
| 9 | SGLT2i and/or GLP-1 RA should be initiated to reduce cardiovascular events, independent of baseline HbA1c and concomitant glucose-lowering medications. |

### Cardiac Rehabilitation

| Statement | Recommendation |
|-----------|----------------|
| 10 | All patients should be offered cardiac rehabilitation as early as possible and no later than 10 days after discharge, whenever feasible. |

### Physical Activity

| Statement | Recommendation |
|-----------|----------------|
| 11 | All patients are recommended to take part in regular aerobic physical activity and resistance exercise. |

### Smoking Cessation

| Statement | Recommendation |
|-----------|----------------|
| 12 | All patients who smoke should be offered smoking cessation therapy as early as possible during hospitalisation. Therapy should be continued after discharge and closely followed up thereafter. |

### Weight and Dietary Management

| Statement | Recommendation |
|-----------|----------------|
| 13 | All patients are recommended to have a normal BMI. |
| 14 | All patients are recommended to maintain a healthy diet, particularly to reduce salt intake, to restrict alcohol consumption, and to avoid trans-unsaturated fats, processed food, and energy-dense foods. |

### Vaccination

| Statement | Recommendation |
|-----------|----------------|
| 15 | Annual influenza vaccination is recommended for all patients with ASCVD. |

## 5. Long-Term Follow-Up Schedule

| Management Area | Minimum Follow-up Schedule |
|-----------------|---------------------------|
| Lipid Management (LDL-C) | At least 4-6 weeks after each dose adjustment/initiation, then every 3-12 months thereafter based on need to assess response or adherence. |
| Diabetes Management (HbA1c) | At least twice a year for patients with diabetes. |
| Left Ventricular Function Assessment | Every 6 months up to 1 year after MI, then at least once a year thereafter. |
| Blood Pressure Management | Every visit. |
| Dose Adjustment/Titration | Every visit or when non-adherence suspected. |

## 8. Implementation Framework

### Key Components for PDMI Protocol Implementation

1. Key Stakeholders
2. Implementation Steps
3. Case Example
4. Recommended Outcome Measures
5. Sustainability Strategies

### Key Stakeholders

#### Clinical Stakeholders
- Cardiologist / Interventional Cardiologist (including fellow and staff)
- Cardiac nurse coordinator
- Clinical pharmacist
- Dietitian / rehabilitation team

#### Administrative & IT
- Medical records team
- Quality improvement unit
- Hospital IT / EMR team

#### External Stakeholders
- Primary care physicians
- Patients/Caregivers

### Roles of Multidisciplinary Team Members

| Role | Responsibilities |
|------|------------------|
| **Cardiologist** | Project lead, clinical oversight |
| **Fellow** | Protocol execution, patient and family education, data collection |
| **Pharmacists** | Review discharge medication list for accuracy, check for drug interactions, counsel patients on medication use and adherence, support optimisation of guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) |
| **Ward Nurse** | Inpatient checklist completion, patient and family counselling |
| **OPD Nurse** | STEMI clinic follow-up, adherence monitoring |

---

## 9. Expected Outcomes

- Reduction in recurrent cardiovascular events and mortality, especially within the first year after ACS
- Increased rate of risk factor control through systematic care processes
- Enhanced treatment adherence and patient engagement through close monitoring from the early stage
- Raised standard of multidisciplinary care, reducing treatment gaps and improving long-term patient outcomes
